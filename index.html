The problem with system changes

Replacing a system is hard. The risks are real, and the cost of getting it wrong is high.

Your contract is ending. You must decide: extend, re-procure, or move. The clock is ticking, and service areas are pulling different ways.

Two suppliers are involved: the old one and the new one. Neither one owns the whole move. Your team is stuck in the middle, trying to keep it on track.

Go-live is a risk. If the data is wrong, or fields are missing, you cannot prove it to auditors or members. The blame lands on you.

A recent project

A UK council replacing its schools system

2 people, 12 months

The council decided to move schools data to a new system. Procurement started late, so they had to go live after just 4 months. We ran testing and live use side by side for the rest of the year.

Fields were matched, tested and signed off while schools were already using the new system. By the end, every field was accounted for and the old system was switched off.

Lesson: Start the move before the contract ends. If you wait until procurement finishes, you have no time left to test properly.

Why system changes go wrong

Most problems start with the data moved into the new system.

  • Old data is messier than anyone thought, and it is found late.
  • The work is bigger than planned, so time and money run out.
  • Fields and codes are matched wrongly, like 1 and 0 instead of Y and N.
  • The people who knew what the old data meant have moved on.
  • Nobody is sure who checked what, or who said yes.
  • Tests are too small, and there is no clear point to say "ready".
  • Nobody counts the records at the end to prove nothing is missing.
  • The switch to the new system has no way back if it goes wrong.

In a survey of over 700 large companies, only 16% of data migration projects finished on time and on budget.

How MapBridge helps

MapBridge is the tool that makes the work faster and leaves a signed-off record.

AI-suggested matches

MapBridge reads field names, types and notes from both systems. It suggests which fields match. You review and approve each one. The approval is recorded by your name.

Code translations

When the old system uses different codes to the new one (like 1 and 0 instead of Y and N), MapBridge spots this and suggests the translation.

Sign-off tracking

For each matched field, you record who sent test data, who checked it, who pushed to live, and who signed it off. Each step is recorded by name and date.

Starter SQL for your supplier

MapBridge writes starter SQL queries for your supplier, with the translation rules already in place. This gives them a head start.

Read-only tool for SQL Server and Oracle

Your IT team can run our read-only tool inside your network. It reads the list of fields (not the data) and gives MapBridge the field names and types. No access to personal records.

For IT and data protection officers

What data moves where, and what the AI mode sends.

What MapBridge reads and stores

Reads: Field names (like employee_id or start_date), field types (like number or date), allowed values (like Y and N), and field notes from your supplier.

Never reads: Employee names, salaries, addresses, or any other personal data. The read-only tool for SQL Server and Oracle only queries the system catalog (the list of tables and fields), not the tables themselves.

Where it is stored: In the web browser on your computer. Not on a server. When you close the browser, it stays on your computer unless you clear your browser data.

What the AI suggestion mode sends

If you use the built-in matcher: Nothing is sent anywhere. It runs entirely in your web browser.

If you use your own AI API key: Only field names, types, and notes are sent to the AI service you choose (like OpenAI or Azure). No employee names, salaries, or any personal data. Your API key is stored in your web browser only.

Common questions

Do you move the data?

No. We help you and your supplier agree which fields match. Your supplier writes the queries that move the data.

Do you need access to our personal data?

No. MapBridge only needs the list of field names and types. It does not need to see any employee names, salaries, or other personal data.

Where is the data stored?

In your web browser on your computer. Not on a server. When you close the browser, the data stays on your computer unless you clear your browser data.

What systems do you work with?

We work with finance, HR, housing, CRM, care and schools systems. The read-only connector supports SQL Server and Oracle. For other systems, we upload field lists as CSV or JSON.

How long does a system change take?

It depends on the size of your system and how much time you have before go-live. A typical project is 6 to 12 months. Start before your contract ends if you can.
